## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy sets out how to use the organisation's devices, accounts, email and social media safely and responsibly. It protects people, information and our reputation.

It applies to all employees, volunteers, trustees, contractors and anyone else given access to our systems or data ("**users**"). It covers:
- organisation-owned and personally owned devices used for our work
- all accounts, systems and cloud services we provide or approve
- work-related use of social media, including personal accounts where the organisation is identifiable or affected

## 2. Core principles

1. Use resources for legitimate organisational purposes.
2. Protect personal and confidential information.
3. Use only the access you are given, and keep credentials private.
4. Be respectful and lawful in all communications.
5. Report problems quickly. Early reporting is always better than concealment.

## 4. Devices

### 4.1 Organisation-owned devices
- Use them for work purposes. Limited, reasonable personal use is allowed if it does not interfere with work, break the law or create security risk [CONFIRM OR RESTRICT].
- Do not let family, friends or other non-users use them.
- Do not disable security tools (antivirus, firewall, encryption, screen lock) or alter system settings.
- Install only approved software. Request anything else from [IT CONTACT].
- Keep devices updated. Install updates when prompted, or within [X DAYS].
- Return all devices and materials on request or when your role ends.

### 4.2 Personal devices (BYOD) [DELETE IF NOT PERMITTED]
- Personal devices may be used only for [APPROVED SERVICES, e.g. email, web apps via browser].
- They must have a current supported operating system, automatic updates, a passcode or biometric lock, and up-to-date malware protection where applicable.
- Do not store organisational files locally unless authorised. Prefer approved cloud storage.
- If a device is lost or stolen, report it immediately. The organisation may remotely remove organisational data and accounts [CONFIRM CAPABILITY].
- Do not use devices that are jailbroken or rooted.

### 4.3 All devices
- Lock your screen when unattended. Auto-lock should be no longer than [X MINUTES].
- Use full-disk encryption on laptops and mobile devices [CONFIRM ENABLED].
- Do not leave devices unattended in vehicles, public places or shared spaces.
- Use removable media (USB drives) only if approved. Encrypt any that hold personal or confidential data.
- Avoid public Wi-Fi for sensitive work unless using the approved VPN [OR DELETE].
- Dispose of devices and media only through [APPROVED PROCESS].

### 4.4 Prohibited activity (any device or network used for our work)
- Accessing, storing or sharing illegal, discriminatory, sexually explicit or violent material
- Installing pirated software or bypassing security controls
- Mining cryptocurrency, running unauthorised servers or scanning networks
- Using the organisation's connections to harass, defraud or infringe copyright

## 6. Email and messaging

### 6.1 Use of email
- Use your organisational email account for all organisational business. Do not use personal email to send or store organisational information.
- Auto-forwarding to personal accounts is not permitted.
- Write professionally. Assume any message could be read by others or disclosed in a legal or regulatory request.
- Use BCC when emailing groups who should not see each other's addresses.
- Check recipients and attachments carefully before sending, especially with personal or sensitive data.
- Send sensitive information only via approved secure methods [e.g. encrypted email, secure link]. Do not send passwords in the same message as the file they protect.

### 6.2 Phishing and malicious content
- Treat unexpected links, attachments, payment or bank-detail changes, and urgent requests with suspicion.
- Verify unusual requests through a separate, known channel (e.g. phone call to a known number).
- Report suspicious messages using [REPORTING METHOD/BUTTON/ADDRESS]. If you clicked or opened something, report it immediately.
- Do not open files from unknown senders or enable macros unless confirmed safe.

### 6.3 Messaging and collaboration tools
Chat, video and collaboration tools ([APPROVED TOOLS]) follow the same standards as email. Do not use unapproved messaging apps for organisational business or to share personal data about beneficiaries, clients or colleagues.

### 6.4 Prohibited email use
- Spam, chain messages, or unauthorised mass mailing
- Harassing, discriminatory, defamatory or threatening content
- Impersonating others or hiding your identity
- Sending marketing without a valid legal basis and required consent or opt-out [CONFIRM AGAINST APPLICABLE LAW]

## 7. Social media

### 7.1 Official organisational accounts
- Only authorised users [ROLE/LIST] may post on behalf of the organisation.
- Official accounts must use MFA, and credentials must be held in the approved password manager.
- Content must be accurate, respectful and consistent with our values and [COMMUNICATIONS/BRAND GUIDELINES].
- Obtain consent before posting images, stories or details of identifiable people, and take extra care with children and vulnerable adults [LINK TO SAFEGUARDING POLICY].
- Respond to complaints or crises according to [ESCALATION PROCESS]. Do not engage in arguments.

### 7.2 Personal accounts
You are free to use personal social media, but:
- Do not share confidential or personal information obtained through your role.
- Do not present personal views as the organisation's. Where your affiliation is visible, consider adding "views are my own", though this does not excuse inappropriate conduct.
- Do not post content that harasses, bullies, discriminates against or threatens colleagues, volunteers, supporters or beneficiaries.
- Do not use the organisation's logo or name to imply endorsement without permission.
- Do not connect with or message beneficiaries (especially children or vulnerable adults) privately unless your role requires it and it is authorised [REFER TO SAFEGUARDING POLICY].
- Follow platform rules and respect copyright, privacy and defamation law.

### 7.3 Security and reputation
- Use strong, unique passwords and MFA on any account that could be linked to your role.
- Report impersonation, hacked accounts, or posts that could harm the organisation to [CONTACT].

## 8. Data protection and confidentiality

- Handle personal data only as needed for your role and according to [DATA PROTECTION POLICY].
- Store organisational data only in approved systems. Do not keep it in personal cloud accounts or messaging apps.
- Do not take screenshots or photos of personal or confidential data except where authorised.
- Print only when necessary. Store paper securely and dispose of it by shredding [OR APPROVED METHOD].
- Confidentiality obligations continue after you leave the organisation.

## 9. Incident reporting

Report **immediately** to [CONTACT/PHONE/EMAIL] if you:
- lose a device or suspect unauthorised access to an account
- clicked a suspicious link, opened a suspect attachment or shared credentials
- sent personal or confidential data to the wrong recipient
- see inappropriate content or behaviour involving our systems or accounts

Do not attempt to investigate or hide an incident. The organisation will assess whether it must notify regulators or affected individuals [e.g. within X HOURS, per applicable law].
