## 5. Risk assessment

Assess risks to **individuals** (not just to the organisation). Consider physical, material and non-material harm, such as distress, discrimination, loss of control, financial loss, reputational damage, loss of confidentiality or exclusion.

**Examples of measures (select those that are relevant):**
- **Technical:** encryption at rest and in transit, multi-factor authentication, role-based access, logging, patching, secure backups, pseudonymisation, automated deletion.
- **Organisational:** staff training, confidentiality terms, clear-desk and device policies, supplier due diligence, breach response plan (72-hour ICO notification where required), data sharing agreements, retention schedule.
- **Transparency and rights:** layered privacy notice, just-in-time notices, simple request process, opt-out options.
- **Design:** data minimisation, avoiding unnecessary free-text fields, human review of automated outputs, testing for bias.

*Guidance: Measures must be real, assigned and resourced, not aspirations. Consider cost and effort proportionately, but cost alone does not justify leaving a high risk unaddressed. Reference the ICO's security guidance and the NCSC's public guidance, such as Cyber Essentials requirements, as benchmarks if suitable.*
