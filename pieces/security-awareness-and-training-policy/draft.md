## 4. Principles

- Training should be practical, relevant and regular, not a one-off exercise.
- Security is a shared responsibility. People are an important line of defence, not the only one. Technical controls should reduce reliance on individual vigilance.
- Reporting is encouraged. Reporting a mistake or suspicious event promptly will be treated as a positive action, and a blame-free approach applies to honest mistakes ([[PLACEHOLDER: confirm alignment with HR/disciplinary policy]]).

## 7. Core Awareness Topics

### 7.1 Phishing and Malicious Messages

Personnel must be trained to:
- Recognise phishing by email, text (smishing), phone (vishing), messaging apps, social media and QR codes.
- Look for warning signs: urgency or pressure, unexpected requests, unusual sender details, mismatched links, unexpected attachments, and requests for credentials, payment or gift cards.
- **Not** click links, open attachments or reply if unsure. Verify using a known, separate contact method.
- Report suspicious messages using the route in Section 11. Where appropriate, forward suspicious emails to the NCSC Suspicious Email Reporting Service (report@phishing.gov.uk) and forward suspicious texts to 7726.
- Report immediately if they have clicked a link, opened an attachment or entered credentials. Do not wait to be sure.

Simulated phishing exercises, if used ([[PLACEHOLDER: yes/no]]), are for learning, not punishment. Results are reported in aggregate, and those who fail receive supportive follow-up.

### 7.2 Passwords and Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Password approach | Use long, unique passwords. Combining three random words is recommended by the NCSC. |
| Reuse | Never reuse work passwords elsewhere or share them. |
| Password managers | Use [[PLACEHOLDER: approved password manager]] to store passwords. Never store them in plain text, on sticky notes or in unprotected files. |
| Forced changes | Do not change passwords on a routine schedule unless required. Change immediately if compromise is known or suspected. |
| MFA | Mandatory for email, cloud services, remote access and administrator accounts, and for any other service that supports it ([[PLACEHOLDER: list systems]]). |
| MFA prompts | Never approve unexpected MFA prompts or share codes. Report them immediately. |
| Device protection | Use screen locks and a PIN, passcode or biometric on all devices. |

### 7.3 Data Protection

Training must cover, in plain language and in line with UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018 and ICO guidance:
- What personal data and special category data are, and examples relevant to the organisation.
- Handling data only for legitimate work purposes, using the minimum necessary, and sharing only with authorised recipients.
- Secure storage, sending and disposal of data (including paper), and using approved systems only.
- Recognising and reporting personal data breaches immediately, including misdirected emails, lost devices and unauthorised access.
- Recognising and passing on data subject requests (e.g. access requests) to [[PLACEHOLDER: contact/role]] without delay.
- Not using unapproved AI tools or personal accounts to process organisational or personal data ([[PLACEHOLDER: confirm position]]).

### 7.4 Social Engineering

Personnel must understand that attackers may impersonate colleagues, managers, suppliers, banks, IT support or officials, and may use publicly available information to appear credible. Training must cover:
- Pretexting, impersonation, tailgating, "CEO fraud" and invoice or bank-detail change fraud.
- Verifying unusual requests through an independent, known channel, especially those involving payments, bank-detail changes, credentials or sensitive data.
- Challenging unknown visitors politely and not allowing tailgating.
- Limiting what is shared on social media and in public about work, systems and colleagues.
- Following the [[PLACEHOLDER: payment/bank-detail change verification procedure]].
- Never disclosing passwords or MFA codes to anyone, including claimed IT staff.

### 7.5 Remote and Mobile Working

Personnel working away from the office must:
- Use only approved devices and services ([[PLACEHOLDER: personal device rules]]), and keep devices updated with automatic updates enabled.
- Avoid unsecured public Wi-Fi for sensitive work. Use a trusted network, mobile hotspot or [[PLACEHOLDER: VPN, if provided]].
- Secure home Wi-Fi: change default router passwords and use strong encryption (e.g. WPA2/WPA3).
- Prevent shoulder-surfing and overheard conversations in public places, and lock screens when leaving devices.
- Keep devices and papers physically secure, and never leave them unattended in vehicles or public places.
- Not use personal email or unapproved cloud storage for work data.
- Report lost or stolen devices immediately so that [[PLACEHOLDER: remote lock/wipe]] can be actioned.
- Follow guidance on travel, particularly overseas ([[PLACEHOLDER: travel rules]]).

| When | Immediately on discovery, and in any case on the same day. Do not delay to investigate or try to fix it yourself. |

## 10. Contractors, Temporary Staff and Volunteers

- Anyone with access to organisational systems or data must complete training appropriate to their access **before access is granted** (or within [[PLACEHOLDER: timeframe]] with restricted access).
- Volunteers receive a proportionate version covering accounts, phishing, data handling, device use and reporting ([[PLACEHOLDER: format]]).
- Contractors and suppliers must either complete [[PLACEHOLDER: organisation]] training or evidence equivalent training. This requirement should be written into contracts or agreements, together with confidentiality and data protection terms ([[PLACEHOLDER: legal review]]).
- Shared accounts must not be used. Each person must have their own account with access limited to what they need, removed promptly when no longer required.
