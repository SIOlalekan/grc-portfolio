## 2. Purpose and scope

**Purpose:** to ensure the organisation keeps records only as long as needed, protects them while held, and disposes of them securely when no longer needed. This supports the UK GDPR storage limitation and security principles, the accountability principle, and wider legal, contractual and business needs.

**Scope:** all records in any format (paper, electronic, audio/visual, messaging, cloud) and all devices and media that hold them. It applies to staff, directors, volunteers, contractors and suppliers handling organisational information.

**Out of scope:** [[PLACEHOLDER: any excluded record types or entities]]

## 3. Principles

- **Justified retention:** every record category has a documented purpose and retention period. Data is not kept "just in case".
- **Personal data:** the UK GDPR does not set standard retention periods. The organisation must set and justify its own, review them, and erase or truly anonymise data when no longer needed (see ICO guidance on storage limitation).
- **Legal minimums respected:** where law, regulators or contracts require a minimum period, that period is applied and its source recorded.
- **Secure throughout:** records are protected from creation to destruction, including during disposal.
- **Evidence:** disposal is recorded so the organisation can show it followed this policy.
- **Single source of truth:** the retention schedule (Section 5) governs. Local practices must not override it.

## 6. Category-specific rules

### 6.1 Personal data
- Link each category to the ROPA and privacy notices. Stated periods or criteria must match what individuals are told.
- Keep only data fields needed. Where only part of a record is needed after the main period, strip the rest.
- Anonymisation must be effective. Pseudonymised data is still personal data. Obtain advice before relying on anonymisation.
- Special category and criminal offence data: apply the shortest justified period and restricted access [[PLACEHOLDER: confirm lawful basis conditions]].
- Periodic reviews: [[PLACEHOLDER: frequency, e.g. annual]] review of held personal data against the schedule.

### 6.2 Business records
- Distinguish **records** (evidence of decisions or transactions) from **transient material** (drafts, duplicates, convenience copies). Delete transient material when no longer useful [[PLACEHOLDER: timeframe]].
- Email and chat: [[PLACEHOLDER: policy, e.g. auto-deletion settings and rules for filing important messages]].
- Archived records must be indexed, access-controlled, and have a review date.

### 6.3 Security logs
- Retain only for as long as needed for detection, investigation and assurance, balanced against privacy (logs can contain personal data such as usernames and IP addresses).
- Logs are protected against tampering and unauthorised access, and expire automatically where possible.
- Logs relevant to an incident or investigation are preserved under Section 7.

### 6.4 Contracts
- Retention is usually driven by the contract end date plus a period linked to potential claims. Confirm the period with legal advice [[PLACEHOLDER]].
- Keep a contract register showing the start, end, renewal and disposal review dates.
- Contracts containing personal data of signatories or contacts follow Section 6.1 as well.

### 6.5 HR records
- Separate files by purpose (recruitment, employment, payroll, health, disciplinary) so each can be disposed of on its own timetable.
- Unsuccessful applicants: tell them how long data is kept. Delete at the end of that period unless consent to retain is obtained [[PLACEHOLDER]].
- Leavers: on departure, apply the schedule and keep a minimal reference record only if justified [[PLACEHOLDER]].
- Restrict access to HR and authorised managers.

### 6.6 Financial information
- Periods must be confirmed against current HMRC, Companies House and any sector-regulator requirements [[PLACEHOLDER: verified periods and sources]].
- Payment card data: do not retain beyond need. Never store security codes after authorisation. Follow the payment provider's requirements [[PLACEHOLDER]].
- Bank details of individuals (employees, sole traders) are personal data. Delete when the retention period ends.

### 6.7 Backups
- Document backup scope, frequency, location, encryption and retention period [[PLACEHOLDER]]. Backups are for recovery, not long-term archiving.
- Backups expire on a defined cycle. Data deleted from live systems may remain in backups until expiry.
- If data cannot be immediately erased from a backup, it must be "put beyond use" (not accessed or used for any decision) until it is overwritten, consistent with ICO guidance. Restores must re-apply any deletions made since the backup [[PLACEHOLDER: process]].
- Backup media and cloud backup deletion follow Sections 8 and 9.
- Test restoration [[PLACEHOLDER: frequency]].

## 7. Legal holds

- **Trigger:** actual or reasonably anticipated litigation, regulatory investigation, subject access/complaint dispute, audit, insurance claim, or security incident. Triggered by [[PLACEHOLDER: role]] on advice from [[PLACEHOLDER: legal adviser]].
- **Action:** issue a written hold notice identifying the scope (people, systems, date range, record types). Suspend scheduled deletion for in-scope records, including automated deletion, log expiry and backup rotation where relevant.
- **Protection:** preserve records unaltered, with restricted access. Keep held personal data secure and limited to what the hold requires.
- **Communication:** notify record owners, IT and relevant suppliers. Record acknowledgements.
- **Release:** only the hold issuer can release it, in writing. Normal schedule disposal then resumes promptly, and any records already past their period are disposed of.
- **Register:** maintain a register of holds (date, scope, reason, issuer, release date).
- **Erasure requests during a hold:** refer to the data protection lead. Document any lawful reason for refusing or restricting erasure.

## 8. Deletion and destruction methods

| Format | Required method |
|---|---|
| Paper | Cross-cut shredding or equivalent on site, or a reputable confidential-waste supplier with a contract and certificates of destruction. Standard: [[PLACEHOLDER: e.g. confirm supplier-stated standard]]. Use locked collection bins. Do not place personal or confidential paper in general waste or recycling. |
| Files/databases (live systems) | Delete in the source system and in linked copies (shared drives, email attachments, exports, test data). Empty recycle/trash areas. Where deletion is logical only, record the residual risk and expiry. |
| Cloud/SaaS | Use the service's deletion functions. Confirm the provider's deletion and retention behaviour in contracts and settings. Delete accounts, shared links and API tokens. |
| Email/messaging | Apply retention settings. Delete from mailboxes, archives and shared inboxes. |
| Removable media | Sanitise or destroy as per Section 9. |
| Third parties/processors | Contract (UK GDPR Article 28) must require deletion or return at the end of services, and evidence on request. Confirm completion in writing. |
| Anonymisation | Only with approval by the data protection lead, and a documented assessment of re-identification risk. |

**Authorisation:** disposal of scheduled records requires confirmation from the record owner that no hold or business need applies. Routine automated deletion is pre-authorised by the schedule.

## 9. Disposal of devices and media

- **Inventory:** maintain an asset register of laptops, phones, tablets, servers, network devices with storage, printers/copiers with drives, USB drives, external disks and removable media.
- **Before disposal, reuse or return** (including leases and repairs):
  1. Check for legal holds and needed data. Back up what is still required.
  2. Remove user accounts and revoke access, certificates and MDM enrolment.
  3. Sanitise storage using a method appropriate to the media type and data sensitivity, following NCSC secure sanitisation guidance. Examples: cryptographic erase for properly encrypted devices, vendor secure-erase or factory reset for mobile devices, and physical destruction for faulty media or the highest risk data.
  4. Verify sanitisation and record the result.
- **Physical destruction:** by an approved supplier with a contract, chain-of-custody and certificate listing serial numbers where possible [[PLACEHOLDER: approved supplier]].
- **Storage before disposal:** hold devices awaiting disposal in a locked, access-controlled location, and log them.
- **Electrical waste:** dispose of or recycle in line with applicable UK waste electrical and electronic equipment (WEEE) requirements [[PLACEHOLDER: confirm obligations and supplier]].
- **Personal devices (BYOD):** [[PLACEHOLDER: permitted? If yes, state requirements for remote wipe and removal of organisational data on leaving or device replacement]].
- **Lost or stolen devices:** report immediately. Remote wipe if possible. Assess as a potential personal data breach.

## 10. Rights requests, incidents and monitoring

- **Erasure and objection requests:** route to the data protection lead on receipt. Respond within the statutory timeframe (generally one month, subject to permitted extension) and see ICO right to erasure guidance. Apply deletion across live systems, and manage backups as in 6.7. Tell recipients of the data where required. Record any lawful refusal reasons.
- **Subject access requests:** do not delete data that is subject to an open request, except routine deletion that would have happened regardless, as advised by the data protection lead [[PLACEHOLDER: confirm approach]].
- **Failures:** wrongly disposed, lost, or unlawfully retained data must be reported under the incident process. Personal data breaches are assessed for ICO and individual notification (the ICO must generally be notified within 72 hours of becoming aware if reporting is required).
- **Disposal log:** record date, record/device description, volume or serial number, method, person, approver, supplier and certificate reference. Retain the log for [[PLACEHOLDER]].
- **Monitoring:** [[PLACEHOLDER: frequency]] spot checks of shared drives, cabinets, mailboxes, backup expiry and supplier certificates. Report findings to the policy owner.

## 11. Exceptions

- Exceptions to a retention period or disposal method must be requested in writing, stating the record, reason, risk, proposed period, and safeguards.
- Approval by the policy owner and data protection lead (and the approving body for high-risk or lengthy exceptions) [[PLACEHOLDER: approval route]].
- Exceptions are time-limited, logged in an exceptions register, and reviewed at least [[PLACEHOLDER: e.g. annually]].
- Exceptions cannot override a legal hold, a legal obligation, or a data subject's rights.
